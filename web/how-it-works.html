How it works

Every eligible room gets a device. We group rooms into zones, give each zone one hub, and run one closed cable loop from the hub through every device and back. Cables run straight and at right angles, stay off the walls and only cross between rooms through doorways and open passages.

The pipeline

Six steps, from a raw drawing to a checked answer.

Read the plan

Rooms, walls, doors, windows and the front door come from the ResPlan drawing (or your uploaded file) and are converted to metres.

Find the free floor and the door links

We work out where a cable may run (inside rooms, clear of walls and windows) and which rooms each door or open passage joins.

Why: zones must be connected through doorways, and cables may only change rooms there.

List every legal zone

Every connected group of rooms within 100 m² and 5 devices is a candidate. From these we also prove the smallest number of hubs any answer can have.

Route a loop for each zone and place its hub

Each loop is routed with an A* search on a grid built from the plan's own corners and doorways, trying the device orders and keeping the cheapest. The hub then goes to the spot on the loop that adds the least cost: that is the heatmap you see in the viewer.

Pick the best set of zones

An exact search picks zones that cover every room with the fewest hubs, then the lowest J. A separate CP-SAT optimisation model solves the same choice as a cross-check, and keeps working on floors far too large for the exact search.

Check it independently

A second program, written without reading the solver's code, rebuilds the walls and door links itself, checks every rule and recomputes L, X, O and B on a 1 mm grid.

Why: if two separate implementations agree on thousands of plans, the numbers can be trusted.
